# Decisions

Dated log of choices and one-line rationale. Newest at the bottom.

## 2026-08-22

- **No Docker on this machine → portable infra.** MySQL 8.4.6 (official Oracle archive zip, `infra/bin/mysql`), Meilisearch 1.x (official GitHub release binary) and Redis 8 (apt, inside the existing WSL2 Ubuntu) are started by `node infra/local.mjs up`. `infra/docker-compose.yml` is the identical stack for machines with Docker. Nothing was installed system-wide on Windows.
- **Next.js 16 instead of the spec's 15.** The copied Welcome / stitch components were written against 16 (`proxy.ts`, React 19.2, Turbopack); it is the current major. No API the spec relies on differs.
- **Queue: BullMQ when Redis answers, MySQL-audited local pool otherwise (`QUEUE_DRIVER=auto`).** Same job semantics (attempts, exponential backoff, per-host politeness); `ingest_items` is the resumability record in both. Redis was reachable, so every run so far used BullMQ.
- **SKU selection is a recorded human+agent step, not a pipeline stage.** Nine research agents chose the 27 flagships from live brand sites and wrote `data/curated/{category}/SELECTION.md` with URL + reason. The pipeline starts from those selections. Live-mode extraction (ANTHROPIC_API_KEY) re-reads the official page and datasheet; without a key the curated files are the fixture provider — same provenance law.
- **Provenance stays honest about prices.** Only 4 of 27 prices could be read from a live listing; the rest are `estimated` with the basis in `price_note` and an "Estimated" badge on card and PDP.
- **GST seeds follow the Sept 2025 rationalisation** (cement 28 → 18 %, solar devices 12 → 5 %); every other category is flagged `needs_verification` until the verify stage confirms it with a key. `gst_rates` is authoritative over the curated file value (conflicts are logged).
- **Calendar sheet: 20 data groups + 4 media headings.** `Images`, `Documents`, `SEO Specs`, `Comparison engine` describe media roles, document types, SEO fields and a compare flag — modelled as `sku_images.role`, `sku_documents.type`, `skus.seo`, `attributes.compare`, not EAV groups. Bulbs attributes are parsed verbatim (431 after de-duplicating repeated labels such as Wattage across groups; first occurrence wins) and typed by `registry/bulbs.overlay.json`.
- **Image gate relaxed to 500 px with a `soft` flag.** Brands publish small renditions (UltraTech hero 580 px, Ceasefire 1000 px). A real photo flagged *re-source for crisp zoom* serves a buyer better than a placeholder; below 500 px the flagged placeholder is used. The coverage report lists every soft and placeholder image.
- **Newage Fire Protection does not manufacture portable extinguishers** (no IS 15683 licence, no extinguisher in its 166-product sitemap). Per the no-invented-models rule, its real extinguisher cabinet CA-EB-01 is listed under the category with inapplicable attributes left empty. Recommendation: swap the third brand to Kanex Fire if a third portable extinguisher is wanted.
- **Philips Ace Saver 9 W B22 is retired from every Signify India property**; values were read from a Moglix listing carrying Philips India's manufacturer block; only one image exists (800 px, soft).
- **USDZ is generated client-side** (three.js USDZExporter from the GLB) when an iOS visitor taps View in your room — no server-side USD toolchain; real GLBs drop in by filename.
- **The AR "View in room" badge is on every card**: Photo Mode works for any product (hero cut-out or placeholder GLB); the asset manifest decides which tier gets true-scale WebXR.
- **Session = signed httpOnly JWT cookie, verified statelessly in `proxy.ts`**; `users`/`sessions` rows exist for audit and logout. Demo OTP is fixed at 000000 and never sent anywhere.

## 2026-08-23

- **CCTV, Glass and Total Stations SKUs chosen by three research agents from live brand sites** (`data/curated/{category}/SELECTION.md`): Hikvision DS-2CE76D0T-ITPFS, Dahua HAC-HDW1200TRQ(S6), CP PLUS CP-USC-TA24L2C-L; Saint-Gobain Sun Ban SGG Sapphire Blue 5 mm, AIS Ecosense Edge Natura Plus 6 mm, Guardian ClimaGuard Blue 5 mm; Leica TS07 2" R500, Trimble C5 2", Topcon GM-52. All 27 files pass the validator with 0 errors; prices are `fetched` for 14 of 27 and `estimated` (with basis) for the rest. Saint-Gobain's domain moved to `in.saint-gobain-glass.com` in `categories.json`.
- **Older curated files brought onto the validator's contract** (numbers stored as numbers, `ai_filled` confidence capped at 0.7) by a one-off script; the pipeline re-ran extract/verify/fill/describe for bulbs and fire extinguishers. No values changed meaning.
- **Image stage never fails a SKU on a bad source**: SVGs are rejected as product photos, and a source sharp cannot decode falls back to the flagged placeholder with the reason in the coverage notes (the cement run had failed three times on an SVG with an unescaped `&`).
- **Calculator engine is pure and versioned**: `packages/estimator` — thumb-rule quantities (`rates/2026-08/quantities.ts`), a city-indexed tier rate card with a `needs_verification` flag on every seed rate, store prices taken live from the catalogue (never city-indexed) by explicit code first, price rank otherwise. 24 hand-computed unit tests pin the reference case (Hyderabad 30 × 40 ft G+1 Medium = ₹34,16,796). Civil labour is shown as its own line at 38 % of civil cost.
- **Drawing upload is a prefill, never truth**: Claude vision (`claude-opus-5`, strict tool schema) reads floors / plot / BUA / door & window counts; without `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` a mock returns a labelled sample from the file's aspect ratio. Either way the UI highlights the prefilled fields and the total is a *preview* until the user taps *Values are right* (save is disabled until then).
- **Saved estimates are recomputed server-side**: `POST /api/estimates` takes inputs only and stores `{ inputs, outputs }` under an 8-char public id → `/estimate?e=<id>`. The client never submits money.
- **3D placeholders are generated, not modelled**: `packages/assets3d` writes a glTF 2.0 binary per SKU with a hand-rolled writer (no three.js on the server) — lathe bulb with E27/B22 cap, dome/bullet cameras, framed solar module, red extinguisher with valve/hose/gauge, tripod-mounted total station, glass pane, tile slab, cement bag, epoxy tins — at the SKU's `dim_*` millimetres. A real `assets/3d/{SKU}.glb` wins by filename; `manifest.json` records `placeholder: false` and the `/3d/*` route prefers it.
- **AR is one engine, three tiers, routed by capability** (`detectTier`): WebXR hit-test (three.js, reticle, tap-to-place, drag, two-finger rotate, light estimation, contact shadow) → iOS Quick Look with a client-side USDZ export → Photo Mode everywhere. Photo Mode without `GEMINI_API_KEY` uses the on-device luminance analyser and asks the user for the room type; the gate, scale and placement maths are shared. The generative composite is Gemini image editing under the fidelity law (soft-histogram + mean-colour score, regenerate < 0.42, overlay wins after two attempts); the mock composite is the overlay itself, labelled. Composite realism is unmeasured without a vision key, so every mock path is labelled as one in the UI rather than presented as a photo.
- **Fidelity metric uses soft (trilinear) histogram bins** — hard 8-bin histograms scored the *same* colour 0 when it fell across a bin edge.
- **Scale proof runs against the production server** (`next start -p 3001`), not Turbopack dev; dev-mode numbers were 10× slower on PDP for compilation reasons, not query reasons. Synthetic SKUs take ids from 1,000,000 upward so a concurrent real ingest can never collide with them (it did once: the 28th SKU landed while the seeder was running).
- **All `(app)` pages are server-rendered per request** because the layout reads the session cookie; ISR tags are kept for the data layer and the revalidate webhook. The measured PDP p95 on the production server decides whether that needs `unstable_cache` around `loadSkuPage` — see `storage/reports/scale-latest.json`.
- **`skus.category_id` is denormalised with two covering indexes** — `(category_id, id)` for keyset category listings and `(category_id, stock_status, id)` for the facet engine's in-stock population. At 400k rows the join-then-sort plan gave the PLP API a 3.09 s p95; the index range gives 73 ms. Migrations `0001`/`0002`; the pipeline and the scale seeder write the column.
- **The facet engine aggregates in SQL** (GROUP BY / MIN / MAX / COUNT DISTINCT against the index-only in-stock id set) instead of streaming every EAV row through the driver: 400k SKUs recompute in ≈ 41 s cold, ≈ 6 s for the largest category, down from 205 s.
- **MySQL buffer pool raised to 1 GB** in `infra/local.mjs` (`my.ini`) — 256 MB could not hold the 1.3 GB `skus` read-model table and every cold facet pass went to disk.
- **PDP p95 is reported at two concurrencies, not tuned to pass one.** With 400k SKUs on the production server the PDP renders in p95 68 ms at concurrency 4 and 233 ms at 10 — the miss is CPU-bound React SSR in a single Node process sharing a laptop with MySQL, Meilisearch and Redis, not a query (all reads are unique-index lookups; a 60 s per-code cache and a 60 s similar-products cache were added). Horizontal instances fix it; the numbers stay honest in `DEMO.md`.
- **Lighthouse SEO on the PDP reads 91 because Next 16 streams `generateMetadata` output into the body for dynamic routes**; the title and description are present in the HTML (verified), just outside `<head>`. Performance and accessibility — the §9 budgets — are 98–99 / 100.
- **The 28th SKU is Ambuja Kawach** (`CEM-AMB-KAWACH50`, official page on ambujahelp.in): one curated file, `pnpm pipeline run --sku`, `pnpm assets:3d` — card, facets, PDP, brand strip, search and AR page appeared with no frontend change.
- **Relative storage roots are repo-root relative in every process.** `MEDIA_ROOT=./storage/media` is read by the pipeline against the repo root, but `apps/web` runs with cwd `apps/web` (`next dev`, `next start`, turbo) and resolved it against cwd — every `/media/*` request 404'd whenever the root `.env` was loaded (which `packages/db` always does). `apps/web/lib/storage-root.ts` now searches upward from cwd for the configured path; `MEDIA_ROOT` and `ASSETS_3D_ROOT` share it.
- **Cache headers live in the route handlers, on 200 only.** `next.config.ts` `headers()` had stamped `public, max-age=31536000, immutable` on every `/media/*` response — 404s included — so a browser that saw a missing image once kept the failure for a year (and did, during the bug above). `/media/*` and `/3d/*` now answer errors with `Cache-Control: no-store`; only `/fonts/*` keeps a config-level header. Anyone who loaded the store before 2026-08-23 morning needs one hard reload (Ctrl+Shift+R).
- **`next/image` loader URLs carry `?w=<width>`.** The rendition ladder (thumb/card/gallery/zoom) never contained the requested width, so next/image's dev check warned on every catalogue image. The media route ignores the query; CloudFront's default cache key does too.

## 2026-08-25

- **The sign-in page's stitched logo is third-party work, kept deliberately.** `apps/web/lib/stitch/` (1,390 lines with `LogoStitchCanvas`) is a reverse-engineered port of the "epoch-stitch" WebGL renderer from `indus.sarvam.ai/login`; its own header records that every constant, shader line and ordering rule is theirs. It is decorative, loads only above 1024 px on `/welcome`, and no other surface depends on it — a viewport that does not get it falls back to the 56 px PNG mark. Reviewed and kept; if the licensing position changes, deleting the directory and the `StitchCanvas` branch in `Welcome.tsx` is the whole removal.
- **Per-process caches are one helper, not six copies.** `loadSkuPage`, `similarSkus`, `allCategories`, `allBrands`, `categoryIdOf` and `loadRegions` each had their own `Map` + `Date.now()` TTL check; two were unbounded and none coalesced concurrent misses, so a cold cache under load fanned every in-flight request straight through to MySQL. All six now go through `apps/web/lib/cache.ts` (`lru-cache`), which is bounded, caches negative results, and does not cache rejections.
- **Number and spec-value formatting lives in `@buildobjects/catalog`.** The "format one attribute value" rule was written three times — the product page, the pipeline read-model, and the facet builder — and had already drifted. The pipeline bakes `spec_json` with the same function the page renders it with, so the two cannot disagree. The formatters are constructed once; `toLocaleString` builds an `Intl.NumberFormat` per call, which a 24-card listing pays for several times over.
- **Delivery dates and stock states were built but never wired.** `deliverBy()`, the `deliverBy` prop on `ProductCard` and the `.deliver` / `.stock--*` markup all existed; no page passed the prop and no stylesheet defined the classes, so neither the promise nor the stock colour had ever rendered. The listing and search pages now pass the session's lead time, and `serviceability()` reads `regions.delivery_days` instead of a hardcoded `regionId === 'hyd' ? 2 : 3` that ignored the seeded column.
- **`app/(app)/loading.tsx` is removed: on Next 16.3.1 it left the entire page body unhydrated.** With a route-level loading file present, the segment's Suspense boundary was emitted with React's postponed marker (`<!--$~-->`) while the completion script the same document carried was `$RC("B:0","S:0")`, which only matches a pending (`<!--$?-->`) boundary. The swap therefore never happened: the real content stayed parked in `<div hidden id="S:0">`, `<main>` kept the skeleton, and no resume request was ever issued. Everything below the header — Add to Estimate, the filter rail, sort, the gallery, quantity steppers, every AR control — was inert in both `next dev` and `next start`; only the header hydrated, which is why the site looked fine. Deleting the file fixes it completely (verified: cards render inside `<main>`, "Add to Estimate" updates the header count, a facet click rewrites the query string). Not caused by the proxy — removing its header mutation changed nothing. If a navigation skeleton is wanted back, put an explicit `<Suspense>` inside the page around the results section and re-test hydration before shipping it; re-check on the next Next.js upgrade.
- **Biome is the whole toolchain: lint, format and import order in one dependency.** There was no linter at all before, which is how `void ne; void sql;` statements and `eslint-disable` comments pointing at a non-existent ESLint survived in the tree. `biome.jsonc` (not `.json`) so every disabled rule states why it is off next to it — `noImgElement` because the pipeline pre-derives exact renditions and a raw `<img>` on one is the intended path; `useSemanticElements` because `role="group"` + `aria-pressed` is what a segmented control actually wants; `noAssignInExpressions` because `while ((m = re.exec(s)))` is the idiomatic form. `useExhaustiveDependencies` declares this project's own hooks (`useCameraStream`, `useOrientation`, `useDismiss`) as returning stable values, so it stops demanding `ref.current` in dependency arrays — React explicitly does not want that, and an autofix that added it broke the camera before it was caught.
- **`pnpm check` is lint → typecheck → test → contrast, and CI runs it plus `build`.** `contrast` was a report nobody ran; it is now a gate, and it earned that on the first run by failing the "Activate BO Engine" label at 3.97:1 against the light end of its own gradient. The colours had been hardcoded in a `.tsx` file where the script could not see them.
- **The BO Coins palette is six tokens, not nine spellings of one amber.** `#f59e0b`, `#fbbf24` and seven different `rgba(245, 158, 11, …)` strings differing only in spacing and alpha were spread across `CartButton`, `AccountMenu` and `BoCart`. They are now `--color-coin*`, `--color-on-dark*` and `--color-engine-*` in `theme.css`, which also means the contrast script can finally see them. Computed values are unchanged.
- **Photo-mode AR is a reducer, not fifteen `useState` calls.** `components/ar/photoSession.ts` owns every transition and `apps/web/lib/photoSession.test.ts` covers them without a DOM. `retake` alone used to set five pieces of state at the call site, and each new call site had to remember the same five. Two bugs fell out of writing it down: the calibrate step had no entry point at all — the reference picker that starts it only renders once you are already in it, so two-tap scale calibration was unreachable — and leaving the live camera on a tier-C device set the mode that was already active, so "Use photo mode" and "Exit AR mode" did nothing on every camera-capable device without WebXR.

## 2026-08-25 · the specification workbook

- **`WHOLE_PRODUCT_LIST_BO_PRODUCT_CALENDAR.xlsx` is now nine sheets, one per category, and it is the source the database is built from.** It was one sheet holding nine cartesian blocks: 24 fixed specification headings × 22 attribute slots = 528 rows per category whether or not the category has such a property. A light bulb carried "Burst Pressure", "Pourability" and "Drainage Flow"; in six of the nine categories the whole "Product identity" column was repeated verbatim under "Manufactring Specifications", "Hygiene specifications" and "Packaging"; 1,302 cells held U+FFFD where an em dash had been destroyed by an encoding round-trip. Nothing in it carried a value. Each sheet now carries one row per specification the category actually has, the value each of the three brands holds for it, and the provenance of each value — 509 specifications and 1,498 values in total. `services/pipeline/tools/build_calendar_workbook.py` performed the migration once; `src/registry/from-sheet.ts` is what reads the workbook from here on, and it is the only thing that does.
- **85.5 % of the attribute values were the string "Verified Industry Standard".** 12,895 of 15,080 values across the curated files were that literal, every one of them stamped `provenance: "verified"` at `confidence: 0.98`. A second family, `standards_and_certifications_parameter_#1` through `#23`, existed only to hold "Certified Architectural Standard". None of it ever reached a page — the registry did not define those keys, so `persistValues` dropped them silently — but it made the sheet look like it held 528 facts per product when it held about 78. The workbook builder refuses both families, and no such value can be written again without appearing in `PLACEHOLDERS`.
- **Specification headings are per-category, chosen in `registry/spec-groups.json`.** Twenty fixed headings applied to every category is what put "Wattage" and "Lumens" under *Product identity*, "Inrush current" under *Flow specifications*, and gave a bulb a *Pressure* section. A bulb now leads with **Light output** (luminous flux, luminous intensity, efficacy, colour temperature, CRI, beam angle), cement with **Strength & structure**, a total station with **Measurement accuracy**, glass with **Optical** / **Thermal** / **Acoustic**. All 28 headings in the vocabulary are in use; none is dead.
- **Luminous intensity is computed, not guessed, and `derived` is a fourth provenance.** The most important number on a lamp was present as the sentence "Wide, near-omnidirectional spread from the frosted envelope rather than a narrow beam" and was not a key spec, so it rendered nowhere. It is now **109.3 cd** on the Philips, 100.7 on the Havells, 95.5 on the Wipro — `I = Φ / (2π(1 − cos(θ/2)))`, the beam's solid angle in steradians. Three more followed the same rule: glass's light-to-solar-gain ratio and shading coefficient, and a solar module's fill factor. A computed value is neither read off a source nor filled from the class average, so it says `derived` and carries its formula in `sku_attribute_values.note`.
- **The seeder now deletes.** It only ever upserted, on the argument that removing an attribute would orphan its values — which is true, and is why 906 attribute rows had accumulated for 509 live specifications and bulbs alone carried 431 definitions against 95 real ones. `pruneCategory` removes what the workbook no longer declares along with its values, so the table cannot drift from the document again.
- **The filter policy names the attribute that carries the distinction, not a placeholder that shares its name.** `product_type`, `grade`, `colour`, `nominal_size`, `coating_type`, `interlayer_material`, `component_system` and `bis_isi_marked` existed only to hold "Verified Industry Standard" and are gone; each facet that named one now names what actually carries the distinction — a camera's `form_factor`, an extinguisher's `extinguishing_agent`, glass's `substrate_glass`, a tile's `body_type`. Thirty-two relabel and conditional values that described the old nine-way `product_type` taxonomy went with them. Which attributes are facets is the policy's decision alone: the workbook's Filter column is generated from it, so the two cannot disagree.

## 2026-08-25 · photographs, and the thirty-seven categories

- **Two-thirds of the product images were not the product.** 89 of the 134 curated image URLs were not on the SKU's own brand domain and about 45 were on a direct competitor's: every UltraTech image pointed at `ambujahelp.in`, every Somany image at `kajariaceramics.com`, Waaree's hero at `adanisolar.com`, Topcon's at `leica-geosystems.com`, Dahua's pack shot at `cpplusworld.com`. Several were Unsplash stock; the AIS architectural-glass SKU showed a mobile-phone screen protector and the Saint-Gobain one a "before/after" window-film advertisement. Only about six of the 28 heroes were right. The URLs are no longer hand-written: `pnpm pipeline images:resource` reads the official product page the fetch stage already captured, ranks the photographs on it, and **refuses anything on another catalogue brand's domain outright** — a rule the ingest stage now repeats as a last gate, because a curated file can still be edited by hand.
- **Three things had to be fixed before a brand's own page would give up its photographs.** Ceasefire's page yielded nothing but 49-pixel Wix thumbnails until `fullSizeVariants()` learned that every major CDN encodes the resize in the URL — strip `/v1/fill/w_49,…/` and the same asset comes back at 1000 px. Somany's asset host answered `406 Not Acceptable` to `Accept: image/*` and `200` to what Chrome actually sends, which is now `ACCEPT_IMAGE`. And a 1000 px hero floor rejected ACC's own 366×528 bag shot, so the floors moved to what Indian manufacturers actually publish and live in one place (`media/images.ts`) — when the re-sourcer and the ingest stage each held their own, the first kept an image the second threw away.
- **Where a manufacturer refuses a robot, a dealer's photograph of the right product is the fallback — never a rival's.** Wipro answers 429 and Saint-Gobain 403, so their pages cannot be captured at all. Those SKUs fall back to the URL a human recorded, but only after the same rival test and a stock-library test, and which images came from a dealer rather than the brand's own host is recorded rather than hidden.
- **The storefront is thirty-seven categories in thirteen departments, nine of them live.** The nine that stock something come first with the count of brands and products behind them; the other twenty-eight are real, clickable places grouped under their department and marked "Arriving soon". They are the sheets of `PRODUCTS LIST.xlsx` that no live category already covers. Departments are the pairing the workbook's own column A used — Electrical Items over Bulbs, Surveying Equipment over Total Stations — and they are what the nav strip lists now: eight words with their categories one hover away, instead of nine category links that stop working at thirty-seven. `registry/taxonomy.json` is seeded into `categories.department` / `categories.status`, so the homepage, the nav, the "All" menu, the rail beside the results and the footer all read one source.
- **A category tile shows what the category is, never a price.** "from ₹410" answered a question nobody had asked at that level and committed the store to a number before the buyer had chosen a product; the tile says how many brands and how many products instead. Live categories are illustrated by the best real photograph among their own products (`pnpm pipeline art:categories`); upcoming ones get a drawn tile in the same light palette. The previous artwork was drawn for a dark ground and had to be run through `filter: invert(1) hue-rotate(180deg)` in CSS to sit on the page at all.
- **The specification sheet reads from the data, not from a table of cement-shaped labels.** `SEGMENT_METADATA` overrode every heading the database supplied with one vocabulary written for cement and applied to all nine categories, so a light bulb was shown "Installation & Curing Instructions" and a total station "Commercial & Wholesale Pricing Slabs"; the quick-filter chips were the same six fixed cement topics, which on a bulb page selected nothing. Headings and their order now come from `spec-groups.json` through the database, the chips are the sections the product actually has, and this file supplies only one mark per heading. The three "key highlight" cards went with it — they duplicated the PDP's own Key details block with a different and worse set of rules — and so did the header claiming the sheet was "Verified" and "Audited", over values that are roughly half filled from the class standard.
- **The buy panel no longer shows a rating nobody gave.** It drew four stars and "4.3" from `skus.rating_placeholder`, a column whose default is 4.3 and whose name says what it is. The word "placeholder" beside it did not undo four gold stars at a glance. It says there are no ratings yet.
- **Twenty-eight category icons were drawn.** `CATEGORY_ICONS` held nine and fell back to `IconCement` for anything else, so every upcoming category in the tree would have shown a cement bag. Same 24-unit grid, same 1.6 stroke, same hand.
- **The hero is chosen by whether it looks like a product, not by where it sat in the ranking.** Filename ranking put every SKU on the right brand's domain and still gave UltraTech a photograph of a city skyline, Saint-Gobain and Guardian a glazed facade, Topcon two surveyors in a field and Safex its own trademark. `studioScore` reads a 32×32 greyscale downsample and scores three things: whether the border ring is uniform (a studio sweep has no detail at the frame edge), whether it is light, and whether the frame is near square. Uniformity carries most of the weight because Adani photograph their cement bags on a dark ground. Measured against the twenty-eight heroes as they stood, scenes scored 0.05–0.35 and product shots 0.55–1.00.
- **A `data:` placeholder in `src` was hiding the real image.** Lazy-loading themes park a transparent GIF in `src` and the URL in `data-src`; taking `src` first therefore found nothing on exactly the pages that need the fallback. Philips and AIS both went from a partial set with a dealer fallback to 5/5 from their own domain once `src` was treated as absent when it is a data: URI. A test caught this before the second full run.
- **Two ratings nobody gave are gone.** The buy panel drew ★★★★☆ 4.3 from `skus.rating_placeholder`, and the brand strip printed an "Engineer rating" of "4.6/5 (typical among structural and repair consultants)" at provenance `ai_filled`. Both are numbers in rows that read as measurements. The panel says there are no ratings yet; the brand strip keeps its fetched facts (founding year, parent company, market coverage) and its hedged qualitative notes, and drops the score.
- **A category with nothing on the shelf says so instead of offering a toolbar.** An upcoming category page rendered the full listing chrome over an empty grid — "0 products · In stock only · Sort by: Relevance", a filter rail with one facet and a "Clear filters" button with no filters set. It now states that the category is in the catalogue and not yet stocked, and links to the nine that are.
- **A path segment is often the only thing separating a product from a category thumbnail.** CP Plus name every asset with a UUID, so nothing in the filename distinguishes their camera from the Wi-Fi router on the same page — and the router, being a larger and cleaner studio shot, took the camera SKU's hero. What does distinguish them is `/prodassets/product/…` against `/prodassets/category/…`, so the ranking now rewards a product path and penalises a category, banner, slider or brand one.
- **There is one source-width floor, and it is 280 px.** Two floors existed briefly — a higher one for the hero, a lower one for the rest — and the hero's had to keep falling: past ACC's 366×528 bag shot, then past CP Plus's 280×200 camera, which is the largest they publish. A rule that keeps moving is not a rule. One number, in `media/images.ts`, read by both the re-sourcer and the ingest stage; anything under `MIN_SOURCE_WIDTH` is flagged `soft` and the zoom lens hides itself.
- **A `_thumb` suffix was costing 1.4 MB of detail.** Dahua serve their camera at `…_thumb.JPG` (8.9 KB, unreadable at card size) and the same asset without the suffix (1.4 MB). Their product page links only the thumbnail, so the CCTV category tile showed a mounting bracket. `fullSizeVariants` now strips `_thumb` / `-small` / `_preview` and friends, which is a convention most content systems share.
- **`sku_images.quality_score` finally holds something.** Images v2 declared it and indexed it and nothing ever wrote it. The ingest stage now records each image's studio score there, and the category tile picks the highest-scoring hero in the category rather than the widest — which is how Cement stopped showing a photograph of a city skyline and Solar Panels a field.

## 2026-08-25 · the last six heroes

- **The hero could only ever be one of the first five images that passed.** Collection stopped the moment five cleared the size floor, so a product shot the ranking put tenth was never downloaded and never considered. UltraTech's page lists six identical carousel photographs of a dam, a road and a skyline before it reaches the bag — the bag is candidate ten. A pool of twelve is gathered now and the five that ship are chosen from it, which fixed UltraTech and Topcon without a hand-written URL.
- **`registry/image-overrides.json` is where a person's decision lives.** Two SKUs needed one. Vikram publish four Paradea renders whose filenames differ only by variant (`paradea_g12_120`, `Paradea-G12_132`, `Paradea-M10_144_550W`) and all score identically against the product name; this SKU is the M10 144-cell 550 W and the pin says so. Dahua's page for HAC-HDW1200TRQ carries a Discontinued_Product banner and one asset under `/ProductImage/` — a PFA13G pole-mount bracket — so its hero is pinned empty and the labelled placeholder renders instead. An accessory shown as the product is the error this whole pass exists to remove; a placeholder saying "image pending" is not.
- **A pin replaces one role, it does not splice the array.** The first version removed the pinned index, which shifted every later image up a slot — pinning Dahua's hero empty promoted a category navigation thumbnail into the hero, the exact opposite of what the pin asked for.
- **Two SKUs were reviewed and left alone, and the file records why.** Fosroc's hero is their own `productDetailImages/024-Large-Fosroc-Epoxy-Grout.jpg`, a base-plate grouting illustration, because that is what Fosroc publish for a pourable grout; the rest of their page is a twelve-flag language switcher. Philips publish no photograph of the Ace Saver 9 W at all — their captured page is a range listing holding a StellarBright 9 W and an Ace Saver 10 W, and the one Ace Saver product link on it now answers 404. A 10 W carton on a 9 W listing is a claim a buyer could act on.
- **The curated files no longer carry the fabrications.** 13,065 values — the "Verified Industry Standard" family, the `_parameter_#N` keys invented to hold "Certified Architectural Standard", and attributes no registry defines — are gone, taking those files from 4.1 MB to 0.6 MB and keeping 1,486 real values. Their `key_specs` lists were resynced to the workbook; they still named `product_type`, `grade` and `mix_ratio`, attributes that no longer exist.
- **`pnpm validate` is part of `pnpm check` and of CI.** The validator already forbade a `verified` value without two sources — it simply was never run, which is how 12,895 of them survived. Wiring it in found three more things: those stale `key_specs`, a `cavity_gas` attribute no registry defines, and a `TypeError` in the validator itself, where a non-null assertion on an unknown key crashed the run before it could report anything else.
- **`assets/3d/photoreal/raw/` is not tracked.** 19 MB of untouched provider downloads for one SKU that nothing reads back. The deliverable is `assets/3d/{SKU}.glb`; `jobs.json`, the ledger that stops the provider being paid twice, stays tracked.

## 2026-08-26 · the north-star rebuild

The irreversible choices from the `design-system/` restyle. Each one closes a door;
the reasoning is here so the door is not reopened by accident.

- **Two type faces added, one retired.** Instrument Serif is the display face and Schibsted
  Grotesk is the UI face; Arimo is gone. The store's ramp ran 12 → 24px, a 2× range, and a page
  whose headline is nearly the size of its caption has no drama in it however correct the rest is
  — that, not the palette, is what made a well-engineered store read as a commodity marketplace.
  Arimo is a Helvetica metric clone and was in the store to be neutral; it succeeded. Four faces
  subset to 192 KB, down from 216 for three, because Schibsted ships as one variable file covering
  400–800 where Arimo needed four static cuts. Measured on the rendered home page: 92px headline
  against a 17px lede, a 5.4× range.
- **Instrument Serif ships WITHOUT its italic.** `fetch-fonts.mts` fetches it; `layout.tsx` does
  not declare it. Nothing in the store sets an italic headline, and a declared source is a preload
  the first paint waits on whether or not a glyph asks for it — the same reasoning that removed
  Encode Sans Light. One line to add back.
- **`scripts/fetch-fonts.mts` exists, and the faces have provenance.** The four faces in this
  repository arrived by hand and had no recorded version or origin. They are fetched from pinned
  paths in google/fonts now, converted TTF → woff2 by fontTools, and their OFL comes down with
  them. The app links `BuildObjectsSans3`, never `Schibsted Grotesk`, so replacing the UI face is
  a change to that file and not a rename across forty stylesheets.
- **The token layer is v2; the v1 names are aliases onto it, and that is the migration.** About
  nine hundred declarations across forty stylesheets speak `--color-ink-2` and `--color-surface`.
  Rewriting all nine hundred by hand to land the same pixels is a nine-hundred-line diff in which
  one typo is a silently wrong colour. Aliasing lands the new ladder everywhere in one edit, and
  each surface moves to the canonical vocabulary as it is restyled. The aliases are semantic, not
  mechanical: `--color-line` and `--color-line-2` both resolve to `--hair`, because an alpha
  hairline is correct on every surface and removes the reason there were two.
- **`--ink-3` shipped at `#96afb4`, not the `#8fa9ae` the token file specified.** The specified
  value measured 4.19 on `--surf-4` — below AA — and surf-4 is a pressed button and a progress
  track, both of which carry secondary labels. The seven-unit lift is invisible as a hue change
  and restores the property this palette's predecessor was retuned to have: every ink is legal on
  every surface, with no pair anyone has to remember to avoid.
- **Nothing hand-writes `-webkit-backdrop-filter` any more.** Lightning CSS keeps both properties
  when the value is a literal and DROPS the standard one when it is a `var()`. Every glass surface
  written the tokenised way was therefore emitting only the prefixed alias, which this Chromium
  does not implement — the header, the palette, the buy column and the price board were all
  rendering unblurred. Writing only the standard property makes the toolchain emit both.
- **`legacy.css` is deleted, not deprecated.** It carried the v1 class vocabulary on thirty
  private token aliases, and v2 made that arrangement impossible rather than merely untidy: it
  defined `--ink-2` as `var(--color-ink-2)`, which v2 defines as `var(--ink-2)`. A custom property
  defined in terms of itself is invalid at computed-value time, so every element it coloured would
  have lost its colour — silently, with no error anywhere. Every call site was rewritten to the
  token it actually meant and the surviving rules moved into `store.css`.
- **`lucide-react` is out of the storefront.** 93 custom glyphs replace it, on a 24-unit grid with
  a 4.6-unit chamfer on every container shape and exactly one accent element per icon. A stock
  icon pack is the fastest way for a visitor to file a site under "template", and this project's
  own brand rules ban one in as many words. The package survives as a devDependency of
  `services/pipeline`, where `category-art-fallback.mts` reads glyph geometry out of it to draw a
  tile for a category that has no photograph yet.
- **The supplied icon file was NOT a drop-in replacement, and saying so is the point.** It was
  advertised as one and was missing `CATEGORY_ICONS`, `CategoryIcon`, `SPEC_GROUP_ICONS` and
  `SpecGroupIcon` — four exports that eight call sites use to render a mark from a string that
  comes out of the database. Dropping it in would have compiled everywhere except the eight places
  it mattered. They are rebuilt against the new set at the foot of `components/icons.tsx`.
- **Search is a ⌘K overlay and the header is one row.** The header was two rows and 104px plus a
  40px category strip — 144px of chrome above every page — and the reason was arithmetic: an
  Audiowide lockup and an 860px search field cannot share a row, so the mark was cut to 22px and
  the store's own name was the least legible thing on the screen. The overlay settles that in
  search's favour rather than against it: the full width of the viewport instead of the leftovers
  of a header row, a 20px input, and room for the scope chips that were a cramped `<select>`. The
  mark goes to 44px, a third larger than it has ever been.
- **`--wm-size` is the MARK's height and the name is set at 0.75 of it.** Driving both from one
  number put "BUILD OBJECTS" at 633px in a 1329px bar. Optical, not arithmetic: a wordmark whose
  capitals match the mark's full height always looks too big, because the mark has margin inside
  it and the letters do not.
- **The rate ticker carries no up/down deltas.** The north star draws them. The store has no price
  history — the catalogue holds one selling price per SKU and the timestamp it was read, and
  nothing anywhere records what cement cost yesterday. A ± beside a number is a claim about change
  over time. A percentage off MRP was the obvious substitute and is worse: printing "−73% off
  list" beside a bulb turns a strip whose job is "these are today's rates" into a strip that
  shouts about discounts, on a store whose whole promise is that the price is the price.
- **`catalogue-aisle.webp` is cropped to its upper 46%, permanently.** `MANIFEST.md` flags it and
  `site-materials.webp` as carrying fabricated packaging — invented UltraTech, ACC, Kajaria and
  Finolex trade dress, some of it misspelled — and records the fix as a defocus it calls "a
  mitigation, not a fix". Checked at native resolution: `site-materials` is clean, and this one
  was not — the mid-ground shelving stayed sharp and the stacks read "… Cement" down the pile.
  The plate ships at 2560px on a public CDN and is the backplate on all 35 category pages and on
  search, so "you cannot read it at the size we display it" is not the test. Blurring fails on
  this composition (the corridor between the two stacks is a seventh of the frame), so it is
  cropped to the ceiling and the lamp run. It is the better plate as well: 3.90:1 against 1.79,
  and every slot it fills is a wide banner.
- **The backplates are derived at stage time, not committed.** Seven 2560px frames become a
  640/1280/2560 ladder in `stage-media.mts`, so one copy of each plate lives in git and a phone
  fetches 18 KB where it would have fetched 766.
